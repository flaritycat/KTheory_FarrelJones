## Definitions

**Definition.** A one-relator complex is a combinatorial \(2\)-complex of the
form
\[
X=(\Gamma,\lambda),
\]
where \(\Gamma\) is a graph and \(\lambda:S^1\to\Gamma\) is an immersion of a
single cycle. This is the complex-level language used by Linton's hierarchy
paper.

**Definition.** A Magnus subgraph of a one-relator complex \(X=(\Gamma,\lambda)\)
is a connected subgraph \(\Lambda\subseteq\Gamma\) satisfying the source
hypothesis that \(\lambda\) is not supported in \(\Lambda\). In the presentation
case \(\langle \Sigma\mid w\rangle\), this corresponds to the familiar Magnus
subgroup generated by a subset of generators that does not contain the full
relator support.

**Warning.** The project should not silently identify every presentation-level
Magnus subgroup convention with every complex-level Magnus subgraph convention.
For first-pass work, use Linton's complex-level convention when applying
Linton's hierarchy theorem.

**Definition.** A one-relator splitting is an HNN splitting arising in Linton's
hierarchy theorem:
\[
\pi_1(X_i)\cong \pi_1(X_{i+1})*_{\psi_i},
\]
where \(\psi_i\) is induced by identifying the associated Magnus subgraphs.

**Definition.** A one-relator tower for \(X\) is a finite sequence of immersions
of one-relator complexes
\[
X_N\to\cdots\to X_1\to X_0=X
\]
whose successive fundamental groups are related by one-relator splittings.

**Definition.** A one-relator hierarchy is a one-relator tower whose terminal
group \(\pi_1(X_N)\) splits as a free product of cyclic groups, in Linton's
terminology. The hierarchy length \(h(X)\) is the minimal length of such a
hierarchy for \(X\).

**Definition.** If \(X_{i+1}\to X_i\) is one step of the tower, the associated
Magnus subgraphs are denoted here by \(A_{i+1}\) and \(B_{i+1}\). They are the
edge-side subgraphs whose fundamental groups are identified by \(\psi_i\).

**Definition.** A one-relator hierarchy is quasi-convex if, at every step,
\(\pi_1(A_{i+1})\) and \(\pi_1(B_{i+1})\) are quasi-isometrically embedded in
\(\pi_1(X_i)\).

**Definition.** For an isomorphism \(\psi:A\to B\) between subgroups of a group
\(H\), Linton defines a \(\mathbb Z\)-stable number \(s\mathbb Z(\psi)\) by
iterating conjugacy classes of intersections and asking how long non-cyclic
classes persist. A one-relator hierarchy is \(\mathbb Z\)-stable if
\[
s\mathbb Z(\psi_i)<\infty
\]
for every splitting map in the hierarchy.

**Warning.** The preceding definition records the usable project shorthand. If
the project later needs to compute \(s\mathbb Z(\psi)\), it must return to
Linton's exact recursive collections \(\mathcal A_i^\psi\) and
\(\overline{\mathcal A}_i^\psi\), not reconstruct them from memory.

**Definition.** A one-relator hierarchy is acylindrical if, for every hierarchy
step, the corresponding action on the Bass--Serre tree is acylindrical.

**Definition.** An inertial one-relator extension is an HNN extension
\[
H*_\psi=\langle H,t\mid tat^{-1}=\psi(a),\ a\in A\rangle
\]
in Linton's source setting, where \(H\) is a finitely generated one-relator
group and the edge subgroups lie inside strongly inert Magnus subgroups.

## Main work

### The hierarchy theorem to adopt

**Source-verified claim.** Let \(X\) be a finite one-relator complex. Linton's
hierarchy theorem gives a finite sequence of immersions of one-relator complexes
\[
X_N\to\cdots\to X_1\to X_0=X
\]
such that each \(\pi_1(X_i)\) is an HNN extension of \(\pi_1(X_{i+1})\) over
Magnus-subgraph data, and \(\pi_1(X_N)\) is finite cyclic.

**Source-verified claim.** In Linton's terminology, each such HNN splitting is
a one-relator splitting, and the sequence is a one-relator tower. Since the
terminal group in the theorem is finite cyclic, the theorem supplies the
project with a first-pass one-relator hierarchy for every finite one-relator
complex.

**Selection.** For `T-001`, the project adopts the preceding source-verified
hierarchy theorem as the canonical hierarchy theorem for the next modules.

### Downstream route vocabulary

**Source-verified claim.** Linton's main equivalence theorem says that, for a
one-relator complex equipped with a one-relator hierarchy, the following
conditions are equivalent in the source setting:

1. the hierarchy is quasi-convex and the whole group is hyperbolic;
2. the hierarchy is acylindrical;
3. the hierarchy is \(\mathbb Z\)-stable and the whole group contains no
   Baumslag--Solitar subgroup.

The theorem also records virtual specialness and quasi-convexity consequences
for connected subcomplexes once one of the equivalent conditions holds.

**Warning.** This main equivalence is a group-theoretic source theorem, not a
Farrell--Jones theorem. Its Farrell--Jones relevance is indirect: it may route
some one-relator groups into already verified known classes such as hyperbolic
groups, finite-dimensional CAT(0)-groups, or virtually special groups if the
needed bridges are separately verified.

**Source-verified claim.** Linton's negative-immersions theorem states that
one-relator complexes with negative immersions have fundamental groups that are
hyperbolic and virtually special, and that their one-relator hierarchies are
quasi-convex.

**Warning.** Negative immersions are not now imported as an active definition
for `T-001`. They are a future route marker for the hyperbolic subtraction
module.

## Proposition / Theorem / Conjecture / Example

**Proposition.** `FJ22` resolves `OQ-027` at first-pass level: the exact
hierarchy theorem adopted for the next `T-001` modules is Linton's finite
one-relator complex hierarchy theorem from *One-relator hierarchies*.

**Proof.** `FJ21` selected Linton's hierarchy paper as the primary modern
one-relator structure source. This module checked the source's definitions of
one-relator complex, Magnus subgraph, one-relator tower, one-relator splitting,
hierarchy length, \(\mathbb Z\)-stable hierarchy, quasi-convex hierarchy,
acylindrical hierarchy, and the theorem labeled `new_hierarchy` in the arXiv
source. The theorem has exactly the structural output needed before the project
can perform residual-gap subtraction: every finite one-relator complex admits a
finite hierarchy by HNN splittings over Magnus-subgraph data, terminating in a
finite cyclic group. Therefore it is the correct first-pass theorem to adopt.

**Warning.** This proposition is a project-organization result. It is not an
`ER-*` established mathematical theorem about Farrell--Jones.

   complex-level setup and the Freiheitssatz role.

   the definitions of one-relator tower, one-relator splitting, hierarchy, and
   hierarchy length.

5. The section introducing \(\mathbb Z\)-stable HNN extensions and the
   \(\mathbb Z\)-stable hierarchy vocabulary.

   hyperbolic hierarchies, acylindrical hierarchies, and
   \(\mathbb Z\)-stable hierarchies without Baumslag--Solitar subgroups.
